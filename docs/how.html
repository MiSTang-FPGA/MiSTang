Cores from an SD card.

A MiSTer swaps the whole FPGA configuration from a Linux HPS. The Nano 20K has none, but it has a Gowin multiboot path and a SPI flash large enough to stage a core.

Measured on the board

Tang Nano 20K, FT2232 serial 2023030621, driven with the same jtag.mjs and gowin.mjs the browser uses.
IDCODE 0x0000081B (GW2A(R)-18) · SPI flash Winbond W25Q64, 8 MiB · 64 KiB read in 0.42 s · SRAM load of 907 418 bytes in 4.8 s (openFPGALoader takes the same 4.75 s) · flash erase, write, verify and erase again on a 4 KiB sector.
RECONFIG_N works from the fabric. Design A, packed with --multiboot_address 0x4F0000, was loaded into SRAM and pulled pin 9 (IOR31B) low after about five seconds. Design B sat in flash at 0x4F0000. Seven to eight seconds after loading, the USERCODE read over JTAG changed from 0x4EAC to 0xFD4B, which is B’s value when loaded directly. No wire was added. The test region was blank and was erased afterwards. Source: hwtest/reconfig.

Why not something simpler

  • Partial reconfiguration: GW2A has no ICAP-style primitive reachable from the fabric.
  • Fabric driving its own JTAG or slave SSPI: the JTAG pins are dedicated and wired to the FT2232.
  • One bitstream with every core: a single core already fills most of the 20 736 LUT4.

Uploading a bitstream from github.io

Chrome and Edge expose WebUSB on HTTPS pages. The board’s debugger is an FT2232 (0403:6010) and channel A is JTAG. Studio speaks FTDI MPSSE directly, then the Gowin sequence: erase SRAM, XFER_WRITE, done; or the JTAG-to-SPI bridge (IR 0x16) for the external flash.

A board that ran Open Pocket GBC holds ROMs and saves across most of this range. Dump the flash before writing.
